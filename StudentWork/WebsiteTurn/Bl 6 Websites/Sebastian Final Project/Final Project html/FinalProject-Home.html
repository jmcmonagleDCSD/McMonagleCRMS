Cars

What are Cars?

Cars are motorized vehicles designed to transport people and goods quickly and comfortably. They come in many shapes and sizes—from compact city cars to powerful sports models—and run on different types of energy, including gasoline, electricity, or hybrid systems. Beyond simply getting us from place to place, cars have become symbols of freedom, innovation, and personal style, shaping the way we live and move every day.

How do they work?

Cars work by using an engine or motor to create power that moves the vehicle forward. In a gasoline car, fuel mixes with air and burns inside the engine, producing energy. This energy turns a part called the crankshaft, which makes the wheels spin through a system of gears. Electric cars work differently—they use a battery to power an electric motor, which directly turns the wheels. No matter the type, all cars rely on important systems like brakes for stopping, steering for direction, and suspension to keep the ride smooth. Together, these parts allow a car to move safely and efficiently on the road.
