History of Teams

The National Hockey League was established in Montreal on November 26, 1917, initially featuring four Canadian teams: the Montreal Canadiens, Montreal Wanderers, Ottawa Senators (original franchise), and Toronto Arenas. The Canadiens are the oldest continuously operating professional hockey team in the world, founded in 1909, and have achieved unparalleled success, winning 24 Stanley Cups and producing legendary players such as Maurice "Rocket" Richard, Jean Beliveau, and Guy Lafleur. The Toronto franchise, which later became the Maple Leafs in 1927, according to ESPN was initially known as the Arenas and then the St. Patricks, has also enjoyed significant success with 13 Stanley Cup victories. The league expanded into the United States in 1924 with the founding of the Boston Bruins, followed by the New York Rangers, Chicago Blackhawks, and Detroit Red Wings in 1926. These six teams formed the "Original Six" era, which lasted from 1942 to 1967. The Bruins won six Stanley Cups, the Blackhawks six, the Red Wings 11, and the Rangers four, with the Red Wings featuring stars like Gordie Howe.


The NHL doubled in size in 1967, adding six new teams: the Los Angeles Kings, Minnesota North Stars (now Dallas Stars), Philadelphia Flyers, Pittsburgh Penguins, and St. Louis Blues, according to NHL.com as well as the California Seals. The Flyers famously won back-to-back Stanley Cups in the mid-1970s, while the Kings have captured two in the 21st century. The Penguins, founded in 1967, later became a dynasty with five Stanley Cups and the iconic Mario Lemieux. Further expansion in 1970 brought in the Buffalo Sabres and Vancouver Canucks, followed by the New York Islanders and Atlanta Flames (now Calgary Flames) in 1972. The Islanders created their own dynasty, winning four consecutive Stanley Cups from 1980 to 1983. The Flames have one Cup victory. The Washington Capitals and Kansas City Scouts (later Colorado Rockies and then New Jersey Devils) joined in 1974. The Devils have won three Stanley Cups. In 1979, the NHL absorbed four teams from the World Hockey Association: the Edmonton Oilers, Hartford Whalers (now Carolina Hurricanes), Quebec Nordiques (now Colorado Avalanche), and Winnipeg Jets (original franchise, later Phoenix/Arizona Coyotes). The Oilers, led by Wayne Gretzky, dominated the 1980s with five Stanley Cups. The Hurricanes and Avalanche each have one Cup win, and the Avalanche have achieved three.


More recent expansions include the San Jose Sharks (1991), Ottawa Senators (current franchise, 1992), Tampa Bay Lightning (1992), Florida Panthers (1993), Mighty Ducks of Anaheim (now Anaheim Ducks, 1993), Nashville Predators (1998), Minnesota Wild (2000), Columbus Blue Jackets (2000), Seattle Kraken (2021), and the Utah Hockey Club (2024), previously the Arizona Coyotes. The Sharks reached the Stanley Cup finals in 2016. The Lightning have won three Cups in the last two decades. The Ducks also won a Cup in 2007. The Vegas Golden Knights, who joined the league in 2017, notably made the Stanley Cup Final in their inaugural season and won the Stanley Cup in 2023.
