What is the NHL?

The National Hockey League (NHL) is the foremost professional ice hockey league in the world. It was formed in 1917 by four Canadian teams. In 1924, the first U.S. team, the Boston Bruins, joined the NHL. By 2022, the league had expanded to 32 teams. The NHL is made up of two conferences, the Eastern and Western, with four divisions. At the end of the regular season, the top three teams in each division and two wild-card teams from each conference qualify for the playoffs. The winners of each conference then compete for the Stanley Cup. The NHL recognizes outstanding player performance through individual awards such as the Vezina Trophy, Hart Memorial Trophy, and James Norris Memorial Trophy.

How the NHL was created

The NHL was created in 1917 following the decision to disband the National Hockey Association (NHA). The NHL was formed by four Canadian teams. In 1924, the Boston Bruins became the first U.S. team to join the NHL. By 2022, the league had expanded to 32 teams.

Teams of the NHL & their Achievements

  • Anaheim Ducks: In 2006-07, became the first California team to win the Stanley Cup.
  • Arizona Coyotes: Relocated from Winnipeg in 1996. Then moved to Utah is 2024 (The Utah Mammoth)
  • Boston Bruins: First American team to join the NHL in 1924.
  • Buffalo Sabres: In 1974-75, made it to the Stanley Cup Finals in just their fifth season.
  • Calgary Flames: One Stanley Cup championship in 1989
  • Carolina Hurricanes: Signed Gordie Howe in 1977 when they were the New England Whalers
  • Chicago Blackhawks: Part of the "Original Six" teams in the NHL
  • Colorado Avalanche: First relocated team to win an NHL title in its first season in a new city (1995-96)
  • Columbus Blue Jackets: Swept the Tampa Bay Lightning in four games in 2018-19, one of the most stunning upsets in NHL history
  • Dallas Stars: In 1998-99, captured their first Stanley Cup championship
  • Detroit Red Wings: 11 Stanley Cups, and a record-setting 23 consecutive home game wins in 2011-12
  • Edmonton Oilers: Acquired Wayne Gretzky in 1978, setting the stage for their dominance in the 1980s.
  • Florida Panthers: In 1995-96, made it to the Stanley Cup Finals in just their third season.
  • Los Angeles Kings: Wins the Stanley Cup in Overtime Game 7 in 2011
  • Minnesota Wild: Joined the NHL in 2000 as an expansion team
  • Montreal Canadiens: Most Stanley Cups with 24 titles.
  • Nashville Predators: Won the President's Trophy in 2017-18.
  • New Jersey Devils: Won Stanley Cup titles in 1995, 2000, and 2003.
  • New York Islanders: Won four consecutive Stanley Cup titles from 1979–80 to 1982–83.
  • New York Rangers: First U.S.-based team to win the Stanley Cup in 1928
  • Ottawa Senators: In 1997-98, upset the top-seeded New Jersey Devils to garner the first postseason series win in franchise history
  • Philadelphia Flyers: In 1974 and 1975, the "Broad Street Bullies" won two Stanley Cups
  • Pittsburgh Penguins: Drafted Mario Lemieux in 1984.
  • San Jose Sharks: In 1993–94, the Sharks shocked the top-seeded Detroit Red Wings, winning their first-round playoff series in seven games in one of the biggest upsets in NHL playoff history
  • Seattle Kraken: The newest expansion team, began playing in 2021.
  • St. Louis Blues: Ended their Stanley Cup drought in the 2018–19 season.
  • Tampa Bay Lightning: In 2018-19, tied an NHL record for most wins in a season with 62
  • Toronto Maple Leafs: 13 Stanley Cup championships
  • Vancouver Canucks: Reached the Stanley Cup Finals in 1982 after finishing the regular season with a losing record.
  • Vegas Golden Knights: Won a conference title in its inaugural season (2018).
  • Washington Capitals: Alex Ovechkin led the team to four straight division titles between 2007–08 and 2010–11.
  • Winnipeg Jets: The Atlanta Thrashers relocated to Winnipeg in 2011.
