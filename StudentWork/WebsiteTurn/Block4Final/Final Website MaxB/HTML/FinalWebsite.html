Hockey

By: Max B

What is Hockey?

Ice hockey is a fast-paced team sport played on ice, significantly influenced by the NHL, where the objective is to score by shooting a puck into the opposing team's net. Gameplay involves face-offs to start play, strategic player positioning with forwards and defensemen, and unique rules like offside and icing that govern puck movement. Players can handle the puck with restrictions, and games are divided into three 20-minute periods, with tie-breaking overtime if needed. Penalties for infractions result in time in the penalty box, creating power play opportunities. The sport features different types of shots, including the powerful slap shot and the quicker wrist shot. A goal counts as a point, with individual points awarded to players involved in the play. Ice hockey uniquely allows substitutions during play due to its demanding pace.

Rules of Hockey

  • Offside: Players cannot pass or receive the puck across two blue lines. Players cannot enter the attacking zone before the puck.
  • Icing: A team cannot shoot the puck from behind its own zone past the opposing team's goal line, unless shorthanded.
  • Handling the Puck: Players can handle the puck but cannot close their glove on it or touch it with a stick above shoulder level. Passing with an open hand is not allowed.
  • Penalties: Minor penalties result in two minutes in the penalty box for infractions such as excessive body contact. Major penalties for violent play result in a five-minute penalty. A game misconduct penalty results in a 10-minute penalty.

History of hockey

Ice hockey began in Canada in the 19th century and quickly became popular in colder regions like Canada and the northern United States, where frozen lakes provided natural playing surfaces. Canada has embraced hockey as its official winter sport and has been home to many of the sport's best players. The Stanley Cup, the trophy awarded to the winner of the National Hockey League (NHL) championship, originated in Canada.

Gameplay of Hockey

The game is divided into three 20-minute periods with a 15-minute break in between. If the game is tied, an overtime period is played, and if it is still tied after overtime, a shootout may occur. Players can substitute during play, which is important because the game is very fast. Forwards typically play for only 90 seconds at a time. Penalties can be given for breaking the rules, such as using the body or equipment to unfairly impede an opponent. A minor penalty puts a player in the penalty box for two minutes, and their team plays shorthanded, giving the other team a power play.
