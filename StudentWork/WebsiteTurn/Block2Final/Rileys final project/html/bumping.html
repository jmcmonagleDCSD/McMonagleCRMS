Bumping and Digging

Bumping is a basic volleyball skill used to receive serves or low balls. You stand with your knees bent and arms straight out, creating a flat platform with your forearms. When the ball comes, you use your legs to push it up gently, aiming to control and pass it to a teammate. Digging is a defensive move to stop the ball from hitting the floor after an opponent’s attack, or low ball. You stay low with bent knees and react quickly, using your forearms to absorb and lift the ball upward to keep the play going.



Digging has different styles depending on the play. The forearm dig uses both arms to control the ball after a hard hit. The one-handed dig is used when the ball is hard to reach with both hands. The dive dig, sometimes called a pancake or do-or-die dig, involves jumping or diving to save a difficult ball. The rolling dig is when a player rolls their body to reach the ball safely. Each type helps keep the ball in play in different situations.



The libero is a defensive specialist in volleyball who wears a different jersey and plays only in the back row. They focus on receiving serves and digging attacks, helping the team keep control of the ball. The libero cannot attack or block but is key to strong defense and smooth passing.
