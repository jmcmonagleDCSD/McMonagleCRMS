Ford's Racing Divisions in the 1960s

Exploring A/FX, B/FX, C/FX, Super Stock, FX, and the Ford Drag Team

A/FX (A Factory Experimental)

A/FX was a class in the 1960s that featured heavily modified factory cars designed for drag racing. Ford was heavily involved, running powerful cars with altered wheelbases and custom modifications to maximize performance. These cars were among the fastest and most innovative in drag racing at the time.

B/FX (B Factory Experimental)

B/FX was similar to A/FX but with slightly different rules, allowing for experimentation with different engines and chassis setups. Ford's B/FX entries often included models like the Mustang and Fairlane, which were modified for increased speed and agility on the strip.

C/FX (C Factory Experimental)

The C/FX class featured smaller or lighter cars with modifications allowed to boost drag racing performance. Ford's presence in this class showcased their versatility in producing vehicles that could be competitive across various racing divisions.

Super Stock

Super Stock was a popular drag racing category where cars retained more factory characteristics but were allowed engine and suspension modifications. Ford’s Super Stock cars were dominant in the 60s, often featuring powerful V8 engines and performance tuning that made them competitive on the national drag racing stage.

FX Division

The FX (Factory Experimental) division encompassed multiple classes, including A/FX, B/FX, and C/FX. These classes pushed the limits of factory car modifications and were essential for Ford's development of high-performance drag racing vehicles.

Ford Drag Team

The Ford Drag Team was a professional racing team that showcased Ford’s commitment to drag racing excellence during the 1960s. With legendary drivers and cutting-edge vehicles, the team helped cement Ford’s reputation in the competitive drag racing scene.

Notable drivers included Ronnie Sox and Don “The Snake” Prudhomme, who became icons for Ford’s drag racing success.
