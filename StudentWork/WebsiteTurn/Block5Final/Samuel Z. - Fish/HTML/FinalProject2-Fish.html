Fish and the facts with swimming


Fish swim by contracting muscles to move their tails and use fins for stability and steering, but they also rely on specialized organs like the swim bladder for buoyancy control or, in the case of sharks and rays, forward motion and their fins to generate lift. The slowest fish in the world is the dwarf seahorse (Hippocampus zosterae), with a top speed of about five feet (1.5 meters) per hour. This extremely slow pace is due to its rigid body and the way it uses a small dorsal fin to move forward, limiting its ability to travel great distances. The fastest fish in the world is the Sailfish, with the ability to reach speeds of up to 68 miles per hour (112 km/h). This title is given to the Indo-Pacific Sailfish due to its remarkable speed, which it uses to outmaneuver prey and evade predators.
